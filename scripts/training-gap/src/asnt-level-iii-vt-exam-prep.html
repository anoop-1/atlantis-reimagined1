The ASNT NDT Level III visual testing (VT) exam is a 90-question, 2-hour computer-based method exam. You need 70% to pass. Paired with the Level III Basic, it earns an ASNT NDT Level III certificate in VT. It tests the science and control behind visual inspection: the physiology of vision, light and lighting, direct and remote techniques, optical aids, borescopes and video systems, measurement, discontinuities in materials and welds, and the codes and procedures a VT Level III writes and approves. Visual testing is the first method applied to almost every component, and at Level III it is examined as a controlled method with its own variables, not as simply "looking".

Who VT Level III exam prep is for

VT Level III candidates come from three directions. Weld inspectors in fabrication and construction already spend their day on visual acceptance and want the ASNT credential that lets them run the VT part of an employer's written practice. Maintenance and in-service inspectors in refineries, power plants and aerospace use remote visual equipment every day (borescopes, videoscopes, crawler cameras) and need the theory of optics, resolution and lighting behind it. Multi-method Level IIIs add VT because their company's written practice lists VT as a method and needs a Level III for it.

Whatever the background, VT candidates share one advantage and one risk. The advantage is that the method is familiar and the equipment is simple, so study time goes into understanding rather than operation. The risk is overconfidence. Candidates who treat VT as the easy method skip vision science, photometry and optics, which are exactly the areas the exam uses to tell a Level III from an experienced Level II. A note on scope: ASNT VT Level III is a personnel certification in the visual testing method under ASNT's programme. It is a different credential from a welding inspector certification issued by a welding society. Some employers ask for both. Atlantis prepares candidates for the ASNT VT Level III exam only.

What the VT Level III exam covers

We organise the ASNT VT body of knowledge, which follows the ANSI/ASNT CP-105 visual topical outline at Level III depth, into six domains.

1. Vision and the eye

Anatomy and physiology of the eye, visual acuity and how it is tested, colour vision, contrast sensitivity, fatigue, and the factors that change what an inspector can resolve: angle, distance, glare, adaptation. Candidates are sometimes surprised by how much of the exam sits here. It underpins why codes specify viewing distance, angle and lighting.

2. Light and lighting

Photometric units (illuminance in lux and foot-candles, luminance), the inverse square law for light, colour temperature and rendering, reflection, glare, shadowing and oblique lighting to reveal surface texture. You should know the minimum illumination and viewing geometry for direct visual examination required by ASME Section V Article 9 and how compliance is measured with a light meter.

3. Direct and remote visual techniques

Direct visual examination with and without optical aids (magnifiers, mirrors), and remote visual examination with rigid borescopes, fiberscopes, videoscopes, pan-tilt-zoom cameras and robotic crawlers. Optics fundamentals appear here: magnification, field of view, depth of field, resolution, and how each is traded off in a remote system. A Level III must demonstrate that a remote system resolves at least what the procedure requires, usually with a resolution target.

4. Measurement and documentation

Weld gauges, fillet gauges, calipers, micrometers, depth gauges, pit gauges, comparators, and measurement on video systems (stereo, shadow and phase measurement). Also documentation: photography, video recording, replication, and what makes a visual record acceptable as evidence.

5. Materials, processes and discontinuities

Discontinuities from casting, forging, rolling, machining and welding, and service damage such as corrosion, erosion, fatigue cracking, wear and deformation. Weld discontinuities get particular attention: cracks, porosity, undercut, overlap, incomplete fusion and penetration, excess reinforcement, misalignment and arc strikes, and how weld profile is evaluated.

6. Codes, standards and procedures

ASME Section V Article 9 for visual examination, construction-code acceptance criteria (the AWS D1.1 visual inspection criteria, ASME B31.3 and API 1104 as common examples), procedure content and demonstration of the procedure where the code requires it.

Where experienced VT inspectors lose marks

  • Vision science. Weld inspectors know acceptance criteria but rarely study the eye. Acuity, colour perception and adaptation questions are often the weakest area on a diagnostic.
  • Photometry. Converting between foot-candles and lux, applying the inverse square law to a lamp, and knowing when a light meter reading is valid all come up.
  • Remote optics trade-offs. Larger field of view means less resolution, and more magnification means less depth of field. Questions test whether you can reason through a camera choice, not just name equipment.
  • Procedure demonstration. Knowing when a code requires a procedure to be demonstrated, and how resolution is proven with a target, is typical Level III content.
  • Service damage. Candidates from new construction are strong on weld discontinuities but less sure on corrosion, erosion and fatigue appearance in service.

Two worked examples of VT Level III reasoning

Lighting a direct visual examination. A procedure calls for direct visual examination of a fillet weld inside a vessel nozzle. The Level III has to confirm three things: that the inspector's eye can get within the viewing distance and angle the code requires, that the illumination at the surface meets the code minimum when measured with a calibrated light meter at the surface being examined, and that glare from a shiny weld cap or shadows from the nozzle wall do not hide surface features. If the eye cannot reach the required distance or angle, the examination is no longer direct. It becomes remote, and the procedure must then show that the remote system provides resolution at least equivalent to direct viewing. The exam frames these as short scenarios: a light reading at one distance, a lamp moved to another, and the question of whether the examination is still compliant. The inverse square law answers most of them.

Choosing a remote system for a boiler header. Access is through a small inspection port, the header is dark and several metres long, and the concern is ligament cracking between tube holes. A rigid borescope gives excellent image quality but cannot turn corners. A fiberscope bends but its fibre bundle limits resolution. A videoscope with articulation, a suitable light source and measurement capability is usually the answer, but the Level III still has to prove resolution at the working distance with a target, set the lighting to avoid washout on bright surfaces, and specify how images are recorded so a finding can be reviewed later. Questions on this topic test the trade-offs, not the brand names.

What a VT Level III does day to day

In a fabrication business, the VT Level III approves the visual procedure, sets lighting and viewing requirements, qualifies inspectors' measurement technique, and resolves disputes over weld profile and acceptance. In maintenance and in-service programmes, the Level III selects remote visual equipment for confined or hazardous access, proves system resolution, defines how findings are recorded, and ties visual results to follow-up by other methods. In every setting, the Level III writes and grades the VT Level I and II examinations under the employer's written practice and signs off training records. VT findings often decide which other methods are applied next, so the VT Level III is also frequently the person who writes the inspection plan. The exam's mix of vision science, optics, discontinuities and code logic reflects that breadth.

ASNT Level III VT exam: frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the ASNT Level III VT exam?

90 multiple-choice questions in 2 hours, per ASNT's Level III examination listing.

What is the passing score?

70%.

Is ASNT VT Level III the same as a welding inspector certification?

No. ASNT VT Level III certifies you in the visual testing method under ASNT's programme. Welding inspector certifications are separate credentials from other bodies. Atlantis prepares candidates for the ASNT exam only.

Does the exam include remote visual inspection?

Yes. Borescopes, fiberscopes, videoscopes, cameras and crawlers, and the optics behind them, are all in the body of knowledge.

Are there calculations on the VT exam?

A few, mostly lighting (inverse square law, unit conversion) and optics (magnification, field of view) questions.

How long should I study?

About 6 weeks at 6 to 8 hours a week after the Basic. Weld inspectors usually spend extra time on vision science and remote optics.

Can VT be my first Level III method?

Yes. Basic plus the VT method exam gives an ASNT NDT Level III certificate in VT.

How is a VT Level III certificate renewed?

Every five years, by 25 renewal points or by retaking the VT method exam.

Can VT Level III preparation be done online?

Yes. The exam is written and computer-based, and our preparation runs live online.
