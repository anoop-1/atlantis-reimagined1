The ASNT NDT Level III Basic examination is a 135-question, 4-hour computer-based exam that every first-time ASNT Level III candidate must pass, alongside at least one method exam. It tests the knowledge a Level III needs regardless of method: how personnel certification works under SNT-TC-1A and ANSI/ASNT CP-189, materials and process technology, NDT training, codes, standards and procedures, and a working familiarity with the other common NDT methods. You need 70% to pass. You take it once. When you renew your Level III later, ASNT asks for method exams or renewal points, not the Basic again.

Who the Basic exam prep course is for

Most people who contact us about the Basic fall into one of four groups. The first is the experienced Level II who has been told by an employer that the company needs its own Level III, usually because an outside Level III is signing the written practice today and the business wants that authority in-house. The second is the Level II who is already doing Level III work in practice, reviewing technique sheets, training juniors and answering code questions, and wants the credential that matches the job. The third is the engineer or quality manager with a degree and one or two years of NDT exposure who qualifies for the shorter experience route and needs to learn the certification and methods material quickly. The fourth is the Level III candidate who already passed a method exam years ago under a different employer programme and now wants the ASNT certificate itself.

What these candidates have in common is that they are strong in one method and uneven everywhere else. A UT Level II who has not touched radiography since a Level I course a decade ago will find the Basic's method-familiarity questions harder than the material and certification sections. A quality engineer will find the materials section easy and the method sections hard. Our prep is built around finding that gap early and spending the study hours there, not re-teaching what you already know.

What the Basic exam covers: the five domains

ASNT groups the Basic content into five areas. The weighting between them is not published as a fixed percentage, so we prepare you for all five and adjust based on your diagnostic results.

1. NDT certification

This is the part most candidates underestimate. You need to know SNT-TC-1A in detail: who is responsible for what, the difference between qualification and certification, how the written practice works, the training and experience tables by level and method, what the general, specific and practical exams are and who grades them, vision requirements, interrupted service, recertification and revocation. You also need ANSI/ASNT CP-189, the standard (rather than recommended practice) version that many US defence and power-generation contracts call up, and where it differs from SNT-TC-1A. Expect questions on ANSI/ASNT CP-105, the topical outlines that training courses are built from, and a working understanding of how a central scheme differs from employer-based certification. Read the documents themselves. Summaries lose the wording that questions turn on.

2. Application of NDT methods

The Basic expects Level II-degree familiarity with the common methods, not just your own. In practice that means knowing, for radiography, ultrasonics, magnetic particle, penetrant, eddy current, visual and the newer techniques, what each method detects, its limitations, the variables that control sensitivity, and when to choose it. A typical question describes a component and a discontinuity and asks which method, or which technique within a method, is most likely to find it. The skill being tested is method selection, the core judgement a Level III is paid for.

3. NDT training

A Level III designs and often delivers training. Questions here cover training programme structure, the relationship between classroom hours and experience, how training records must be kept, and what makes training acceptable under a written practice. Candidates who have trained juniors informally find this section fair. Candidates who never have can learn it in two focused study sessions.

4. Materials science and process technology

This is the largest body of reading. It covers the structure and properties of metals, heat treatment, and the manufacturing processes that create discontinuities: casting, forging, rolling, extrusion, welding, machining, plating and heat treatment. It also covers service-induced damage such as fatigue, stress corrosion cracking, creep and hydrogen damage. The questions link process to flaw: which discontinuity does this process produce, where does it sit, how is it oriented, and so which method finds it. ASNT's Materials and Processes for NDT Technology is the standard text, and it is dense. We break it into weekly chunks tied to the method-selection questions above, so the reading earns marks twice.

5. Codes, standards, specifications and procedures

A Level III writes and approves procedures, so the Basic tests how the documents fit together: code versus standard versus specification, how a code references a standard, what a procedure must contain, and how acceptance criteria are applied. You will not be asked to recite ASME Section V clause numbers from memory. You will be asked what a procedure has to specify and who has the authority to approve it.

Employer sponsorship: why companies pay for the Basic

For an employer, a Level III is a dependency. Under SNT-TC-1A the employer's written practice needs a Level III to approve procedures, administer and grade exams, and sign off certifications. Companies without one rent that authority from an outside Level III, which works but leaves the programme dependent on someone else's calendar. Sponsoring an internal candidate through the Basic and a method exam is how many inspection companies bring that authority in-house.

A sponsored candidate still needs the employer's own specific examination and appointment under the written practice. The ASNT certificate is the core credential. It does not replace the employer's programme. We help with both sides: exam prep for the candidate, and a written practice review so the new Level III has a programme ready to run. ASNT Level III Basic exam: frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the ASNT Level III Basic exam?

135 multiple-choice questions, with 4 hours to complete them. ASNT lists these figures on its NDT Level III examination page.

What score do I need to pass the Basic?

70%. The same passing score applies to each ASNT Level III method exam.

Do I have to pass the Basic before a method exam?

A first-time ASNT NDT Level III certificate needs both the Basic and at least one method exam. Candidates schedule them in different orders. We usually recommend the Basic first, because its method-selection and materials content supports every method exam that follows.

Do I need to retake the Basic when I renew my Level III?

No. ASNT Level III certificates are valid for five years and renew by points (25 are required) or by retaking the method exams. The Basic is not repeated at renewal.

How long should I study for the Basic?

For a working Level II studying 6 to 8 hours a week, we plan 10 weeks. Candidates with an engineering or materials background can often finish in 6 to 8 weeks. Single-method technicians usually need the full 10 weeks.

Is there a practical exam at Level III?

The ASNT NDT Level III Basic and method exams are written, computer-based exams. Your employer's written practice may add its own specific exam, and some also require a practical demonstration, before appointing you as the company's Level III.

Can I prepare for the Level III Basic online?

Yes. The Basic is a knowledge exam, so live online preparation fits it well. Atlantis runs the sessions live online, with recorded replays, timed practice sets and optional method refreshers on the Practical NDT simulator.

What is the difference between SNT-TC-1A and CP-189 on the exam?

SNT-TC-1A is a recommended practice that the employer adapts in its written practice. ANSI/ASNT CP-189 is a standard with mandatory requirements, and it is often invoked by contract. The Basic expects you to know both and to recognise where their requirements differ.

Does passing the Basic make me a Level III for my employer?

Not on its own. The ASNT certificate requires the Basic plus a method exam. Under SNT-TC-1A, your employer then certifies you as its Level III under its written practice, usually after its own specific examination.
