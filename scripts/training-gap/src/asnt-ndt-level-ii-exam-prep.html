The ASNT NDT Level II is ASNT's own central certification for Level II technicians in MT, PT, RT, UT and VT, and it meets Recommended Practice SNT-TC-1A. Candidates pass a 50-question, 2-hour General exam and at least one 40-question, 2-hour Specific exam in the General Industry or Pressure Equipment sector. The employer still carries out the vision, practical and any job-specific exams its written practice requires. The certificate renews every five years. ASNT's older ACCP programme stopped taking new applications in May 2023 and was replaced by ASNT 9712, a separate scheme built to ISO 9712:2021. What the General and Specific exams test

General exam (per method)

The General exam follows the CP-105 Level II topical outline for the method. For UT that means wave physics, transducers, calibration, straight- and angle-beam techniques and interpretation. For RT it covers radiation physics, exposure, film and digital recording, image quality and safety. For MT it covers magnetism, currents, techniques, particles and demagnetisation. For PT it covers penetrant materials, process steps and interpretation. For VT it covers vision, lighting, optical aids, remote visual equipment and discontinuities. Fifty questions in two hours gives you plenty of time. The difficulty is breadth, especially for technicians who learned one application on the job.

Specific exam (per sector)

The Specific exam is built around an NDT procedure. You are tested on applying a procedure to the equipment and techniques of the General Industry or Pressure Equipment sector: setting up, calibrating, performing the examination and recording results as the procedure requires. The skill being tested is reading a procedure precisely and doing exactly what it says. Technicians who work from habit rather than from the document lose marks here.

How the ASNT NDT Level II fits your employer's SNT-TC-1A programme

The most common misunderstanding about the ASNT NDT Level II is that it replaces the employer's certification. It does not. It supplies the written part of the evidence, independently. A typical sequence for an employer using the credential looks like this:

  1. The employer's written practice is updated to say that the ASNT NDT Level II General and Specific exams will be accepted as the written general and specific examinations for the methods concerned.
  2. The technician completes the formal training hours the written practice requires, often at least ASNT's published minimum for the method, and documents them.
  3. The technician accumulates and documents the required experience in the method.
  4. The technician passes the ASNT General and Specific exams at a test centre.
  5. The employer's Level III administers the vision examination and the practical examination on real specimens, plus any job-specific exam.
  6. The employer certifies the technician as Level II under its written practice, referencing the ASNT certificate in the personnel file.

Done this way, the employer's audit file carries both an external credential and the employer-controlled elements that SNT-TC-1A expects. If your written practice does not yet mention the ASNT exams, a Level III needs to amend it before the credential can count. Atlantis can do that as part of a written practice review.

Where Level II candidates lose marks

  • Breadth outside their application. A pipeline UT technician may never have done straight-beam forging work. A field MT technician may never have used a wet bench. The General exam covers the whole Level II outline.
  • Reading the procedure literally. The Specific exam rewards doing what the procedure says, not what you usually do on site.
  • Formulas. UT and RT candidates who rely on instrument calculations lose easy marks on Snell's law, near field, inverse square and exposure questions.
  • Terminology. Indication, discontinuity and defect have different meanings. Relevant and non-relevant indications are defined terms. Loose field usage costs marks.

ASNT NDT Level II exam: frequently asked questions

Is ACCP still available?

No. ASNT stopped accepting new ACCP applications on 15 May 2023 and stopped renewals on 30 August 2023. ASNT 9712 replaced it, and the ACCP programme sunsets fully by September 2028.

What is the difference between ASNT NDT Level II and ASNT 9712 Level II?

ASNT NDT Level II meets SNT-TC-1A, and the employer performs the practical and vision exams. ASNT 9712 is built to ISO 9712:2021, requires practical exams at ASNT authorised exam centres, and has its own training and experience minimums.

How many questions are on the ASNT NDT Level II exams?

The General exam has 50 questions in 2 hours. Each Specific exam has 40 questions in 2 hours.

Which methods are offered?

Magnetic particle, liquid penetrant, radiographic, ultrasonic and visual testing.

Which sectors do the Specific exams cover?

General Industry and Pressure Equipment.

Who gives the practical exam?

For the ASNT NDT Level II, your employer carries out the visual acuity, practical and any job-specific exams required by its written practice.

How long is the certificate valid?

ASNT NDT Level II certificates are renewed every five years.

Does passing make me certified to work?

You still need your employer's certification under its written practice. The ASNT certificate is portable evidence of knowledge. Authorisation to perform and sign inspections comes from the employer.

Does Atlantis prepare candidates for ASNT 9712?

No. Atlantis trains to ASNT SNT-TC-1A only and prepares candidates for the ASNT NDT Level II exams.

Can I prepare online?

Yes. The written exams are computer-based and our preparation runs live online. The practical is completed on real specimens with your employer, or with Atlantis onsite at your facility.
